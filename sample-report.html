01 — Your question

“Should we buy a house next year, or kill the credit cards first?”

Neither, exactly. You should spend the next 90 days fixing the thing underneath both of them — and then the house answers itself in about two years instead of never.

Here's the honest version. You earn $6,800 a month after taxes, which is a real income. After everything goes out, you keep $208 of it. That's 3% of what you make. At that rate, a $20,000 down payment is eight years away and the credit cards never actually die, because every time you get ahead of them something breaks and they absorb it.

The debt isn't your problem. The debt is a symptom. Your problem is that $495 a month is leaving the house through channels you'd close today if you could see them. Section 3 shows you exactly where. Once that's fixed, everything else — the cards, the buffer, the house — stops being a question of willpower and becomes a question of arithmetic.

The answer

Don't buy in 2027. Target month 22–26.

Buying next year would mean draining a savings account you don't have yet, at a debt-to-income ratio that will get you either denied or a rate you'd regret for thirty years. Follow the sequence in Section 6 and you arrive at a mortgage application with a $20,000 down payment, no credit card balances, a credit score roughly 40–60 points higher, and a payment you can actually carry. That's a genuinely different house and a genuinely different life. It's worth the extra year.

05 — What we're not doing

And why not.

A plan is defined as much by what it refuses. Each of these is something you asked about or would likely be sold in the next six months.

Not consolidating the cards into a personal loan.

The offers you're seeing quote 14–18% with a 5% origination fee — about $525 up front to move $10,500 around. It lowers the payment, which feels like progress, while extending the term and leaving you with two empty credit cards and the same spending pattern that filled them. Fix the $495 leak first; the leak is what created the balance.

Not investing beyond the employer match.

You asked about opening a brokerage account. Not yet. You're carrying 24.9% debt — paying it down is a guaranteed, tax-free 24.9% return, and no market projection beats a guarantee. The match is the one exception because it returns 100% instantly. Come back to this the month the Visa hits zero, and we'll mean it.

Not buying a house in 2027.

At $1,042/month in debt payments against $6,800 take-home, your back-end DTI puts you in the range where you're either denied or approved at a rate that costs you tens of thousands over the loan. And a purchase next year would require spending a down payment you'd have to borrow, leaving zero buffer on the day you acquire a roof that can leak. This isn't "no." It's "in about 22 months, from a position of strength."

Not hiring a credit repair service.

There is nothing on your report to repair — no collections, no late payments in four years. Your 694 is card utilization sitting at 71%. Get that under 30% and the score moves on its own, free. Anyone charging you $99/month to "fix" this is charging you to wait.

Not touching the 401(k).

You mentioned the loan option. Borrowing from it to clear the cards converts unsecured debt into debt that becomes due in full if Marcus loses his job — which is precisely the moment you'd be least able to pay it. It also stops the compounding on the balance. The answer is no, and it stays no.

07 — Outside our lane

Three things that need a licensed professional.

We'll always tell you when something is beyond what we do, and we don't take a fee for saying so.

  • The student loans → a student loan specialist or your servicer directly. Dani's employment may qualify for an income-driven plan or forgiveness track. That's a determination with real legal consequences and we won't guess at it. Start with your servicer; it's free.
  • Your tax withholding → a CPA, before next filing. You received a $3,100 refund last year, which means you lent the government about $258/month interest-free. Adjusting your W-4 could put most of that in your paycheck now, where this plan can use it. A CPA should confirm the number.
  • Wills and guardianship → an estate attorney. You have two children under eight and no will. This has nothing to do with money and it is the most important item in this report. A simple will in Ohio typically runs $300–600. Please don't wait for the debt to be gone.
